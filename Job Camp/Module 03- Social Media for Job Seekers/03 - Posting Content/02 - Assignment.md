## Guide to making content on LinkedIn

When making content on LinkedIn there are some guidelines you should keep in mind. These Guidelines are set in order to make your content stand out from the rest.

**They key thing to focus is Thought Leadership.**

The reason why we make content is to enhance your personal brand. A great way to do this is to position yourself as a thought leader in the Web3 Industry, don’t just make content on topics everyone else does (topics on challenges like Ethernut are way overdone), instead focus on topics that discuss future trends, potential challenges, and your vision for the industry, demonstrating your expertise and forward-thinking mindset.

Apart from these here are a few more guidelines to make sure your content fits your personal brand

1. **Avoid Posting Assignment Content:**
   - Its good practice to refrain from using your school or bootcamp assignments as LinkedIn content, we say this cus a lot of people on LinkedIn do this. It’s not unique and may not resonate with a broader audience. Aim to share insights and experiences that showcase your individual expertise and perspective.
2. **Research Topics Relevant to You and Your Audience:**
   - Invest time in researching topics that not only interest you but also provide value to your audience. Consider current trends, emerging technologies, and industry challenges that your network might find intriguing and informative..
3. **Protect Your Intellectual Property:**
   - Never share full source code as part of your LinkedIn content. While it might be tempting to demonstrate your skills, sharing code publicly can lead to theft or misuse. Instead, discuss your approach, challenges faced, and the solutions you implemented without revealing proprietary code.
4. **Share Personal Experiences and Learnings:**
   - Your unique journey in the Web3 space is valuable. Share personal stories, lessons learned from your projects, and how you overcame specific challenges. This not only adds a personal touch but also establishes you as an authentic and relatable voice in the industry.
