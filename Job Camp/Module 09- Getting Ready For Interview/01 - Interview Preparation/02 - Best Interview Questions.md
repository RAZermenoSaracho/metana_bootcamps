# Best Interview Questions

When it comes to interviews, the recruiter isn’t the only one who’s allowed to ask questions. You get the opportunity to shoot your questions to the recruiters, and this is one of the best opportunities you get to understand more information about the company and the job. ?

Another reason to ask this question would be to mitigate the feeling of being interrogated, especially if you feel anxious or nervous during interviews. Asking questions can help develop a better conversation between you and your interviewer. ?️

## What would you say are the most important qualities you’re looking for in a person for this role?

A job description might list requirements or desired qualifications, but this question can open up the answer to revealing more about the role’s priorities. The team might be looking for somebody who will execute tasks or a self-starter who will bring fresh ideas to the table.

This can help you determine if you’re a fit for the position and give you clues about what skills you should emphasize and what stories to prepare in a future interview.

## What’s the salary range for this role?

A phone screen presents an opportunity for both the candidate and the employer to discuss salary expectations. Companies typically have a predetermined salary range for the roles they are looking to fill, and it is acceptable for the candidate to inquire about this range. In fact, recruiters may ask you about your desired salary for the position.

Before the interview, research the typical salary range for someone in that role, taking into account your experience, location, and expectations. Come prepared with a fair salary range for your position. If the recruiter asks about your desired salary, you can provide this range. You may also use your previous salary as a reference point and indicate that you expect an increase.

If the recruiter does not bring up compensation during the interview, don’t hesitate to ask about it. Although discussing salary can feel uncomfortable, establishing expectations upfront ensures that neither party wastes time if the offered salary is too low for the candidate. The recruiter can also confirm that your expectations align with their budget.

## Why is the position open?

Now this is one of the Best questions you can ask ( My personal Favourite )

Asking why an organization is hiring for a role can give you useful information about career progression opportunities, the organization’s direction, and what situation you’ll be walking into should you accept a job offer.

The previous person in the role may have moved on to a different job, been fired, or been promoted to a new position. In any case, you’ll have a better idea of the dynamics and opportunities in that role. If the position is entirely new, ask why the position was created. You’ll have better insight into whether you’ll be filling a role with clear definitions or one where flexibility will be an asset.

## What is your organization’s remote work policy?

Remote work has become more common in recent years, and many companies are offering more remote or hybrid positions. It can be worth clarifying what a company’s remote policy is and whether the company offers any flexibility if, for example, your child falls sick or you want to spend time with family across the country.

Even if the job requires you to be in an office, companies may allow employees to work remotely a few days out of the year.

If you’re interviewing for a remote position, you can inquire about how the company fosters a sense of inclusion and belonging for remote workers within the company culture. Additionally, you could ask whether there are opportunities for remote employees to meet their coworkers in person throughout the year, such as company-wide events or gatherings.

## Can you tell me about the interview process and timeline?

This question clarifies expectations around when you can expect to hear back about the next steps and what you should anticipate in interviews to come. The recruiter may share the names or LinkedIn profiles of the people you will be interviewing with and layout how many interviews the process typically includes.

This can also help determine if the process will fit into your timeline. If you’re looking for a job to start immediately, but the position requires you to go through security clearance that will take several months, this will be crucial information to have.

## What do people in this role typically go on to do?

By knowing what people in the role have gone on to do, you’ll build context around the skills you’ll acquire in the role, whether it seems like a transitory role, and if the organization has a culture of promoting people internally. If this is a new role, you might ask what the expectations are for somebody who has been in the position a year from now.

## How would you describe the team that I’ll be working with?

This question can shed some light on the specific team you’ll be working with day-to-day. Some teams within companies can have their own cultures, so it’ll be a good idea to ask about the team and the larger organization. Hopefully, you’ll walk away with an understanding of how big the team is and how you’ll fit in, in addition to some details like typical working hours and the direction the team is moving.

## What is a typical day like in this role?

While the recruiter may not know all the nitty-gritty details of what the team does day-to-day, this question can still give you an inside scoop on the company’s culture, work hours, and how they approach work-life balance. They might also have some info on specific projects or programs you’d be working on, and who your coworkers would be.

Sure, the recruiter isn’t directly managing the team, but they can probably give you a decent overview of what the work environment and responsibilities are really like, as well as the kind of people you’d be working with if you take the job.
