# Research

**Once you have a sense of what you’re looking for in a career, the next step is to do some research. This could involve:**

🚀 Exploring job roles to see what interests you.

🚀 Researching the qualifications and experience required for different roles.  
  
🚀 Seeking out mentors or informational interviews with people who work in the fields you’re interested in. (Reach out to professionals in LinkedIn, Reddit, or Twitter).  
  
🚀 Reading books, blogs, or articles to gain insights and knowledge about different career paths.

**🚀** Research what the market is paying for the role you’re going after.  
  
☣️ Resources: [Levels.fyi](http://levels.fyi/), [Glassdoor](https://www.glassdoor.com/Salaries/index.htm), [Salary.com](http://www.salary.com/), [Comparably Salary Tool](https://www.comparably.com/salaries)  
  
🚀 Tip: Factor in total compensation, too, so that you know how to intelligently negotiate on the back end and what variables you can negotiate (such as equity, bonus, sign-on, etc).  
  
🚀 Tip: Knowing your value in the open market also allows you to know what to say when a Recruiter or Hiring Manager asks you: “What’s your salary range?”  
  
🚀 Tip: Once you know your range, use this script the next time a Recruiter/HM asks you about your salary range

Study 15 to 25 job descriptions of the role you’re going after so that you get a clear understanding of what keywords to include in your resume.  Remember, research is a crucial step in finding the right career fit. It helps you gain clarity and make informed decisions about your professional journey.
