# Following up on quality applications

Now that you know how to submit quality applications, **time to deep dive into app follow-up.** When you submit an app your work isn’t over! You need to try to get your app seen by a human so it doesn’t languish

### **Fast facts about follow-up:**

- **Follow-up means contacting a person who works at the company you are applying to.** You might hear the Careers Team refer to this as “outreach” or “cold contacting” as well.
- **Lambda grads get the best results from follow-up emails.** But you can also connect with someone via a LinkedIn connection request + with an attached note, a LinkedIn InMail message, a Twitter DM, etc.
- **Your top priority when you outreach is to your “future boss” or the technical stakeholder for the role.** If you can’t find this person, find a “future peer” or someone who holds the same title as you on the team. Bonus points if they have a similar pathway into tech.

The most important thing is that you make contact with an actual person involved with hiring for the role you’re applying for. Don’t skip this step – **outreach is unavoidable in a successful job search!**

Want more context behind why it’s important to follow up on a quality application? Take a look!

### **The ATS is, sadly, not your friend.**

To understand why we outreach, it’s useful to understand exactly how an Applicant Tracking System works. When you apply to a role, your app lands in a database of many other names. Because no one has time to review every single app submitted for a job the hiring team needs to use the ATS to cut the list to a manageable size.

Teams can filter on things like years of experience, location, or things that were listed as “nice to haves” in the description. It actually doesn’t really matter what a hiring team filters on, because **you simply can’t customize your resume for every filter out there.**

### **Outreach is your friend!**

Instead of customizing your resume for every possible filter a hiring team might use, it’s more efficient to use outreach to get yourself on the hiring team’s radar. Don’t wait for an ATS to surface you, your outreach increases the chance someone will pull up your app.

No matter where you are in the ATS, filtered out or not, they can search for your app. Outreach is an important tool to advocate for yourself in the hiring process. Get around the ATS, and do your best to ensure that the time you spent on your quality application doesn’t go to waste.

### **What about my quality application?**

You might be thinking — hold on. I spent time on my application, submitted it through the company site, applied within 24 hours of the role being posted, I’m a 50% match for what they are asking for.

Won’t the team know my name by those steps alone?

…maybe. Sometimes you’ll get invited to interviews without outreach, especially when you’re among the first to apply. But in your job search, you want to operate on more than maybes! Outreach increases the potential for someone to see your quality application.

## Next Steps

Now that you know why it’s important to ensure your application is seen by a human, it’s time to implement the most crucial step.

☑️ Send an outreach note to talent or technical professional at the company

Use the applied column in your job tracker to pick the opportunity that needs a follow-up. Contact a hiring or technical professional who works at the company you applied to by bringing attention to your application with a simple LinkedIn connection note or email.

**Message a recruiter or talent professional at the company saying:**

> Hi [THEIR NAME],
>
> While researching [company name] I noticed you’re a [recruiter//talent title] for them. If you have time this week, can we chat about how my skills with [mention skills from job description] match the open [role title] role I applied for? I’ve attached my resume for your convenience.

or

**Message a technical professional saying:**

> Hi [THEIR NAME],
>
> While researching [company name] I noticed you’re a [Software Engineer//Data Analyst] for them. If you have time this week, are you open to chatting about your experience at [company]? I recently applied for the [role title], so I’d love your insights.

This note is simple and asks a question that hopefully prompts a response. The hope is that by sending off such a note, the receiver is curious enough to look up your application. We’ll dive deeper into outreach and message templates like the ones above later in this course.

### **Bonus Tip : Try to get on the Radar**

Once you have connected with the developers working for the companies that you like to join, take a look at their linkedin profiles to see if they post contents and if they do

You can start commenting on their posts to offer valuable insights or pose thoughtful questions  
Engaging with content posted by other developers is crucial for gaining visibility among their other followers, including potential recruiters or business employers. One of the main reasons these developers post content is to engage readers. Due to this they will likely appreciate your interaction and be more inclined to network with you
