# Informational interview and asking for a referral

## Overview

All outreach messages drive towards a simple ask: “Are you free for a quick phone chat this week?” When your contact is open to chatting – what happens next? First, congratulate yourself on a job well done! An informational interview with an engineer, recruiter, or anyone who works at a company you are applying to (or interested in) is essentially like giving yourself a phone screen. You’ve advocated for yourself and given yourself a chance to become more than a resume to your potential new team. ?Well done!

### **➡️ You have two goals with your informational interviews:**

**Goal #1 is to make a new friend, simple as that!**

- This call is a chance to get to know a new person in tech, and ask a lot of questions about who they are and how they got to where they are.
- Remember that people love to talk about themselves and, most importantly, people love getting invited to talk about themselves. [Links to an external site.](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/positive-prescription/201703/why-we-love-talking-about-ourselves)!
- Below are some sample questions to ask your contact, but to strategize questions specific to your contact, schedule time with a coach here. [Links to an external site.](https://calendly.com/lambda-outcomes)!
  - **Sample questions to get people talking in an informational interview ⤵**
    - How they got to where they are/how they got started at the company
    - A typical “day in the life” at their current role
    - What were they prepared for in working there, what they were underprepared for
    - What makes a great {**`role that you're applying for`**} or what are they looking for in their next hire OR their next teammate if they hold that role.
    - Favorite technology/process at their job currently
    - Specifics to the company and what you’ve found in your research i.e. “I’ve noticed that Bloom is growing like crazy right now! How do you keep up with all that new traffic while still considering legacy customers and features?”

**Goal #2 is to drive the conversation towards a referral.**

This is secondary to **Goal #1** because people won’t refer people they don’t like.

Before hopping off, make sure you get a chance to ask one (or both!) of these important questions, which work to drive the conversation towards internal referrals.

⭐ **I recently applied to the open {`Role Title`} at {`Company Name`} and I am even more excited about the potential of working at the company after chatting with you, which I didn’t think was possible. How would you recommend I stand out in the application process?**

- If you get more of a *“study up on code challenges”* than a *“let me check in with my boss for you!”* or *“let me see if I can get you a referral link”* move on to the next question.

⭐ **Is there anyone else you would recommend I talk with at the company who is involved in hiring for this role?**

- *If they share a name, make a note of it, and if it doesn’t feel like too much to ask your new friend, see if they are able to introduce you to this person directly.*

If you don’t get much from either question, never fear! An informational interview is never a waste of time. You’ve just met a new contact in the tech field (and made a new friend!). Tech hiring is very network-based. If this person gets contacted by a recruiter in the future or sees another role in their network, it is very possible they’ll think of you for it. This is another reason to make sure you set a ? reminder to follow up with them in the 2-3 months and keep the conversation going!

## Next Steps

### Send a thank you letter!

> Hi (name), Thank you so much for taking the time to meet with me today to talk about your experience at {company name}. After learning more about (share one or two takeaways from the interview about the company/team’s priorities), I’m even more excited about the team and the problems you are solving! {Optional note about something you chatted about/inside joke/article that relates, etc.} I really appreciate you offering to touch base with your boss about me and my resume — if there is anything that I can clarify about my experience or qualifications, please don’t hesitate to reach out! Thank you for your consideration, (your name)

No matter what comes of the talk, make **a reminder** to follow up with this person in the next few months. Consider sending them an article about something related to your conversation or a quick snapshot of a project you are working on. See if they need any support on anything, as appreciation for them taking time to chat with you a few months ago.

Keeping connections alive is as important as making new ones. You never know what being top of mind can do for you in the future!
