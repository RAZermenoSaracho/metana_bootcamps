# Time to follow up on outreach

## **Leverage Your Network**

Go through your existing professional relationships to see if you’re connected to someone within the organization of interest. If such connections exist, kindly ask them to forward your application to the relevant hiring manager, or request for an introduction to the recruitment officer.

Struggling with connections within the company.

## **Identify the Recruitment Officer’s Contact Information**

First, look for any contact details provided in the job listing. If you found the job on a job board and there’s no direct email listed in the job ad, visit the organization’s website to see if you can find the email address of the hiring manager. If all else fails, a quick call to the company’s main line to ask can often solve the problem.

Next…

## **Compose a Follow-Up Email to the Hiring Manager**

Use an explicit subject line, for example: Checking in on my application for [job title].  
Maintain courtesy and modesty in your email body.  
Express your continued interest in the role and reemphasize why you are an ideal candidate.  
Ensure your follow-up email is concise.

## Initiate a Phone Call

Start by inquiring if it’s a suitable time for the hiring manager to have a conversation with you. If not, ask for a more convenient time to call them back.

An earlier study indicates that while 46% of hiring managers prefer to be contacted via email, 39% are open to telephonic follow-ups. The reason?

Professionals receive an overwhelming number of emails daily (approximately 122).

Your application and subsequent follow-up emails might become lost in the hiring manager’s inbox. A brief phone call can help bypass this obstacle.

Now, what’s next?

## Avoid Persistency to the Point of Annoyance

Unless the hiring manager has not adhered to a specified response deadline, refrain from following up more than twice. It’s crucial to realize when a company might not be interested and to move forward accordingly. Daily calls or emails or, worse, in-person visits to inquire about your application status will likely deter the company from considering you.

By the way, ignoring your follow-ups is quite unprofessional. If a company does not display respect at this stage, it’s unlikely they will once you join them.

Remember, there are numerous opportunities out there, so…

## Maintain Your Job Search

Even if you believe you’ve discovered your ideal job, keep applying to other companies while awaiting a response.

Do not fixate on a single job listing. Despite being an outstanding candidate, there might be uncontrollable circumstances that prevent you from being successful. Stay open and keep your options broad.
