# Proof Of Work

### **Your certificate is worthless.**

This might be a controversial topic. First, let’s start by saying it’s good to take certification courses and boot camps because you learn valuable knowledge, but this is just one part of it.

As a developer, you need to apply what you’ve learned on projects (these can be open-source projects that you do on GitHub), and as mentioned before, these projects need to be shared as content on LinkedIn. This will help recruiters improve the credibility of your certifications.

It’s important that you present a project you’ve done using a tech stack rather than presenting only certificates to recruiters; this will improve your chances of getting selected.

### **Why are there so few Junior developer roles**

Business owners have to pay premium prices to hire junior developers through a recruiter. Take a minute and think, if you were a business owner, would you pay a high price for a recruiter just to hire a junior developer when there are thousands of fresh graduates and out-of-work developers everywhere? I think we both know the answer to that.

So, rather than depending on recruiters to find you a job, do these following things instead:

1. Learn what technologies are used by businesses, learn these technologies, and approach the companies.
2. What open-source projects can you contribute to in order to master your skills?
3. Network with the developers working at the company you want to work for.

After you have done these three tasks above, you can make these companies hire you without having them pay a hiring fee.

### **How do you choose a side project?**

When it comes to choosing projects, choose something that can interest you for the next couple of months. Most importantly, recruiters will look into your projects, and what you need to focus on is the tech stack you use and how you overcame challenges.

Focus on tutorials and use that knowledge to build better projects that you would like to work on.

### **Highlighting your Skills in Group Projects**

Doing group projects are totally valid as they reflect your teamwork skills as well. However, it’s important to share what you specifically did on these group projects because recruiters aren’t hiring your entire friend group; they’re simply hiring you. So, you need to explain your role and contributions in the projects.

When explaining a group project to a recruiter, it’s important to share details on:

1. What did you learn?
2. What challenges did you face?
3. Where did you go wrong?
