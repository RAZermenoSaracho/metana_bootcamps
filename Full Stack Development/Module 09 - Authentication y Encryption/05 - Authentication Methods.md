# Authentication Methods

In web applications, various methods exist to authenticate users, each serving different purposes and offering distinct advantages. While we won't delve into every authentication method, it's essential to grasp their names and meanings to navigate the digital landscape effectively. Let's explore some common authentication methods,

1. **Username & Password:** The foundational method where users enter their unique credentials to gain access.
2. **Cookies-based Authentication:** Involves creating a session ID upon login, which is stored in a cookie on the user's device to maintain their authenticated state.
3. **Token-based Authentication:** Utilizes unique signed tokens to grant access or permissions, often stored in cookies, local storage, or session storage, typically valid for a limited duration.
4. **OAuth & OpenID Connect:** Facilitates single sign-on and allows users to authenticate using existing accounts from third-party services like Google or Facebook.
5. **Single Sign-On (SSO):** Enables users to log in once for access to multiple related systems, enhancing convenience and user experience.
6. **One-time Passwords (OTP):** Requires users to log in using a temporary code, often delivered via text message or email, enhancing security especially for actions like password recovery.
7. **Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA):** Adds extra layers of verification, such as codes sent to mobile devices, to strengthen security beyond just passwords.

While all these methods are relevant, our primary focus will be on **username & password, cookies-based authentication,** and **token-based authentication.** Additionally, understanding **one-time passwords** is valuable for scenarios like password recovery and account security.

While **OAuth and OpenID** are significant technologies in the industry, we'll cover them only briefly due to their complexity, reserving in-depth exploration for later stages of your career.
