# Discretionary Access Control (DAC)

### **Ownership-Based Access**

- DAC operates based on ownership of resources, allowing owners to control access to their resources.
- Owners have the authority to grant or revoke access to their resources as they see fit.

### **Flexibility and Decentralization**

- DAC offers more flexibility and decentralization compared to other access control methods.
- It allows for granular control over resource access, empowering owners to manage permissions independently.

### **Use Cases**

- While not as common in web applications, DAC is frequently used in filesystems, such as Linux/Unix environments.
- File system permissions in Unix systems often use DAC, where owners can set permissions for `owner`, `group`, and `everyone` categories, defining actions like reading, writing, and executing files.

#### **Examples**

- On a personal computer, users can set permissions on their files and folders, determining who can access, modify, or execute them.
- In collaborative document editing tools, document owners control who can view, edit, or comment on their documents.
- In shared project management platforms, project owners regulate access to project-related data, deciding who can access and modify it.

DAC provides owners with autonomy over their resources, enabling them to tailor access permissions according to their specific requirements.
