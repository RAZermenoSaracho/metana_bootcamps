# Authentication vs Authorization

Authentication and authorization are often mixed up, but they're actually different concepts with distinct meanings.

### Authentication: "Who You Are"

Authentication is all about confirming someone's identity. It's like proving you are who you claim to be. Here's what it involves,

- **Verifying Identity:** Making sure that the person claiming to be "John" is indeed "John."
- **Individual Verification:** Ensuring that only one person can claim a specific identity.

When a site verifies that you are "John" by checking your username and password, it's authenticating you. But it doesn't decide what you can do yet; it just confirms your identity.

### Authorization: "What You Can Do"

Authorization comes into play after authentication. Once your identity is confirmed, authorization determines what you're allowed to do. It's like granting or denying access to certain things based on who you are. Here's what it's about,

- **Granting Access:** Deciding whether you have permission to access specific resources or perform certain actions.
- **Resource Access Rights:** Determining what rights you have, like viewing or editing information.

After you're authenticated as "John," authorization decides whether you're allowed to access certain pages or features on the site.

#### Understanding Check

When you log into a site using your username and password, and it checks if your details match what's stored, is this authorization or authentication?

***Answer:** This process verifies that you are who you claim to be. So, it's **authentication**. It confirms your identity before deciding what you're allowed to do on the site.*

Understanding the difference between authentication and authorization helps us navigate digital systems more securely and effectively, ensuring that only the right people access the right resources.
