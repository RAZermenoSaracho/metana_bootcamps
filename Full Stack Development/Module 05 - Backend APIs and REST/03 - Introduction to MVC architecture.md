# Introduction to MVC architecture

Let's breakdown what MVC is in the context of a Restaurant :

1. **Model (Chef and Kitchen):**
   - In a restaurant, the chef and the kitchen staff represent the Model. They are responsible for preparing the food, managing recipes, and ensuring that the ingredients are fresh and of high quality. The Model in MVC is concerned with handling the data and business logic of the application, just like the chef manages the core aspects of the food preparation.
2. **View (Waiter and Presentation):**
   - The waiter taking orders and serving dishes to customers corresponds to the View. The View is responsible for presenting the information to the user in a way that is understandable and visually appealing. Similar to how a waiter communicates between the kitchen and the customer, the View interacts with the user and displays the data provided by the Model.
3. **Controller (Manager and Coordination):**
   - Now, imagine a restaurant manager who oversees the overall operation, communicates with the kitchen (Model), and ensures that the waitstaff (View) delivers the correct orders to the customers. This managerial role represents the Controller in MVC. The Controller handles user input, processes it, and interacts with both the Model and View to ensure that the application functions smoothly.

So, in the restaurant analogy:

- The **Model (Chef and Kitchen)** manages the data and business logic.
- The **View (Waiter and Presentation)** presents information to the user.
- The **Controller (Manager and Coordination)** handles user input, processes it, and manages the flow between the Model and View.

This separation of responsibilities in MVC architecture helps to create a more organized, modular, and maintainable structure for software development, just as it does in the efficient operation of a restaurant.
