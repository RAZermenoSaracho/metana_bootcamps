# Adding JS to a HTML page

![](https://resources.metana.co/public/60/7e/607e0f8a4136a419399d60071600103e20787dec4ea8dd84fabb27167bf65f92.png)

Imagine you have a magic notebook (HTML page) that can do all sorts of cool tricks, but it needs a little help from a wizard (JavaScript) to perform some special tasks. Here's how you can tell your magic notebook to work with the wizard:

1. **Meet the Magic Notebook (HTML)**

- Your magic notebook is like a piece of paper where you can write down instructions for your computer. It's special because it can understand certain codes. So this means your HTML

![](https://resources.metana.co/public/11/0e/110e6cc800f5b58cf04590490aca1825a99f186bee8c11d293d8704f530d4a2d.png)

2. **Inviting the Wizard (JavaScript)**

- Your wizard (JavaScript) is the one who can perform magical spells. To invite the wizard, you need to write a special note (script) inside your magic notebook.

![](https://resources.metana.co/public/07/22/07229fb871f4b5ae546e34576ad93c8a211b7f5b608cd74c8053ab4991ce7e01.png)

3. **Giving Instructions to the Wizard**

- Now, you can write down instructions for the wizard inside the script. For example, you might want the wizard to make a message appear on your magic notebook.

![](https://resources.metana.co/public/94/25/942533fc97ecd57e6146fce870c1dcb0aa40cc803a7bdb11614b54c6a7ef06a6.png)

Now, when you open your magic notebook in a web browser, it will show a message saying, "Hello, I am your wizard!" That's it! You've just added a JavaScript spell to your HTML magic notebook. The wizard will follow your instructions whenever someone opens the notebook in a web browser.
