# Agile Development vs Waterfall Method

### Waterfall Method

The Waterfall Method is a linear and sequential approach to software development. It follows a rigid step-by-step process where each phase (requirements, design, implementation, testing, deployment) is completed before moving on to the next. It's often compared to a waterfall flowing downwards, with each phase cascading into the next.

### Agile Method

The Agile Method is a flexible and iterative approach to software development. It emphasizes collaboration, adaptability, and customer feedback. Agile projects are divided into small, manageable iterations called sprints, allowing for continuous improvement and adaptation throughout the development process.

### Key Differences Between Agile and Waterfall

- **Approach**: Waterfall follows a sequential approach, while Agile is iterative and incremental.
- **Flexibility**: Agile is more flexible and adaptive to changes in requirements, while Waterfall is less accommodating to changes once the project has started.
- **Customer Involvement**: Agile encourages regular customer involvement and feedback, whereas Waterfall typically involves less customer interaction until the later stages of development.
- **Delivery Time**: Agile often delivers software in shorter cycles, while Waterfall may take longer to deliver a fully functional product.

### Pros and Cons of Agile and Waterfall

**Agile**

- *Pros*: Allows for flexibility, faster delivery, customer involvement, and continuous improvement.
- *Cons*: Requires strong collaboration and communication, may be challenging to implement in certain environments.

**Waterfall**

- *Pros*: Provides a clear roadmap, well-defined requirements, and structured approach.
- *Cons*: Less flexible, limited room for changes, potential for longer delivery times, and less customer involvement until later stages.
