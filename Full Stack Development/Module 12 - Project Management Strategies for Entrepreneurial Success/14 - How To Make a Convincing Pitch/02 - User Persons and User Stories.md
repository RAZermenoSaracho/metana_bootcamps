# User Persons and User Stories

### What are user personas and user stories ?

- At the end of the day, the end user of your products and services are always people. We use stories to help visualize how those people would interact with your product.
- When defining our ideas user stories and user personas are tools to communicate who might use your product, how it would help them, and what features and behaviors they would encounter.
- This helps communicate the idea clearly, and allows you to focus on important features for development.

### What are user stories ?

- A user story is a story of how usage of the project would work. These give concise **descriptions of desired functionality** from the **user's perspective**. They typically follow the format: "As a [user role], I want [goal] so that [benefit]."
- User stories describe how someone would use your product, what features and actions would have to happen.
- This helps someone understand how your product would be used, and it helps you to define *what features* you need to develop to accomplish those behaviors.

### What is a user person ?

- A user person is an example fictional user. User personas are used in our stories, to help imagine the people using our software.
- Describes fictional but representative profiles of target users, including their demographics, behaviors, needs, goals, pain points, and preferences. User personas help in designing user-centric solutions.
- It can be concrete.
  - Example: “Bob is a 53 year old construction worker from the US. He has a an associates degree, owns his home, and three adult children. He wants to learn to code to be more in touch with modern technology, and learn something to keep his mind active. He’s frustrated by trying to figure out how to code on his own, and wants an easy to follow step by step guide”

### Using these in pitches

- Adding in example user stories and user personas into your pitch deck makes your story come alive.
- People following your idea can suddenly imagine using it themselves, or imagine the people who would pay to use your product.
- Include at least one person and story in your pitch.
