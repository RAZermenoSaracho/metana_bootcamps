# Development Sprints

### **Using sprints for your development stage**

- At this point, we’ve gotten through collecting and vetting your ideas, turning them into actionable plans, and pitching them. Now we get into actually building it.
- We’ll use a tool borrowed from Scrum and Agile methodology called a sprint.
- We’ve previously discussed Agile methodology in the previous section — now you’ll apply it to build your own project.

### **Steps to plan your development sprint**

1. Plan out your major **project features**.
2. Assign those features into **milestones** — these are major, high-level features and goals to accomplish to get an MVP of your project built.
3. Distribute those milestones onto a **timeline**, based on the time you can invest in building your project.
4. Divide up each milestone into sub-tasks — these are concrete, small, actionable steps you can take, which would each take anywhere from a couple of hours to 1-2 days to develop.
5. Put it all into a Kanban board — then you’re ready to get started with development.

### **Planning your time**

- Keep it short — Allow yourself 1-10 weeks maximum for a single sprint.
- Use cycles — If the sprint goes on too long, it will lose momentum. You should divide it up into smaller sprints. We call these cycles.
- Working iterations — Try to build a working model each time: a version of the app or product you could actually use by the end of the sprint, even if it’s basic and has limited functionality.
- Rest and review — after each sprint, take time to rest, recuperate, rethink, and review. You’ll have a better idea of where to direct your energy with some thought and review, so take at least a couple of days between sprints.

### **What is a sprint ?**

- A sprint is a short, time-boxed period when a development team works to complete a set amount of work.
- You could have a sprint to build out the base of your project or add a single feature.
- Sprints have a definite duration, end date, and goal.
