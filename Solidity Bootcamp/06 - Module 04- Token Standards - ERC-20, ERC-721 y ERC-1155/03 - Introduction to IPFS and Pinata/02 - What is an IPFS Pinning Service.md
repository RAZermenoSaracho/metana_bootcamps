# What is an IPFS Pinning Service

This tutorial of IPFS 101 explains what an IPFS Pinning Service is and how it simplifies decentralized file storage for Web3 developers, highlighting tools like Pinata for efficient and reliable content persistence.

[Video: What Is An IPFS Pinning Service?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=98bg9Q3vqdU)

**Transcript for the AI**

Hello everyone, and welcome back to IPFS 101! Today, we’re focusing on a critical component of IPFS: pinning services. So, what exactly is an IPFS pinning service, and why should you care? Let’s dive in. Uploading files to platforms like Google Drive is straightforward, but IPFS, or InterPlanetary File System, operates differently, and that’s where pinning services become essential, especially for Web3 builders prioritizing security, cost efficiency, and reliability. Unlike the centralized HTTP model, IPFS runs on a decentralized and distributed network, akin to a vast system of local post offices rather than a single central post office. This setup ensures faster, more efficient, and resilient file sharing. Now, let’s get to the core of IPFS pinning services. Running your own IPFS node is like operating your own post office—it demands significant power and resources. Pinning services, such as Pinata, act as an ideal middleman, offering a seamless user interface to upload files to IPFS as easily as you would to Google Drive. With Pinata, after signing up, you simply click upload, select your file or folder, and Pinata handles the rest, ensuring your files are always accessible anytime, anywhere. You can also integrate Pinata’s API for greater control over your files.

Another great feature of Pinata and IPFS is metadata handling—uploading metadata is as simple as uploading any file, requiring only the CID, the unique identifier IPFS uses to locate content. In summary, IPFS pinning services simplify your journey into the decentralized web by providing a user-friendly way to upload and manage files on IPFS, and Pinata has you covered.
