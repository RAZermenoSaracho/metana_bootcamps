# Differences between Ethereum and Bitcoin

[Video: Bitcoin OR Ethereum: What Makes Them DIFFERENT?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=siWX317ALgA)

Transcript for the AI

The video begins with a fun, boxing-style introduction, presenting Bitcoin as “digital gold” and Ethereum as the “decentralized computer for the world.” While often portrayed as competitors, the video emphasizes that the two are fundamentally different in purpose and functionality. Bitcoin, launched by the mysterious Satoshi Nakamoto, was designed as a peer-to-peer digital cash system to store and transfer value without intermediaries. In contrast, Ethereum, introduced six years later, extended blockchain’s capabilities by enabling smart contracts and decentralized applications (dApps). Ethereum’s goal is not just to serve as a currency but to provide an open platform for innovation.

The video compares the consensus mechanisms: Bitcoin uses Proof of Work (PoW), where miners solve puzzles to validate transactions, while Ethereum has transitioned to Proof of Stake (PoS), where validators stake ETH to earn rewards. In terms of supply, Bitcoin has a fixed cap of 21 million coins, while Ethereum has no hard cap, though its issuance has declined and can even become deflationary depending on network activity. At the time of the video, Bitcoin’s market cap is over $366 billion, and Ethereum’s is around $155 billion. Ethereum is also faster, producing blocks every 10–20 seconds, compared to Bitcoin’s 10-minute intervals. Both networks face scalability challenges but are adopting layer-2 solutions, like Lightning Network for Bitcoin and rollups for Ethereum, to improve speed and usability.

Ultimately, while Bitcoin and Ethereum are often compared, the video concludes that they are not true rivals but rather serve complementary roles in the broader effort to decentralize the global economy. Each addresses different problems and contributes uniquely to the blockchain ecosystem.

| **Feature** | **Bitcoin** | **Ethereum** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Purpose** | Digital currency, store of value | Platform for decentralized apps (dApps) and smart contracts |
| **Origin** | Launched in 2009 by Satoshi Nakamoto | Launched in 2015 by Vitalik Buterin after an ICO |
| **Monetary Policy** | Fixed supply of 21 million coins | No fixed supply; issuance can change with upgrades |
| **Consensus Algorithm** | Proof of Work (PoW) using SHA-256 | Proof of Stake (PoS) |
| **Block Time** | 10 minutes per block | 15 seconds per block |
| **Smart Contracts** | Limited to basic contracts (e.g., multi-signature) | Supports complex smart contracts and dApps |
| **Transaction Model** | UTXO (Unspent Transaction Output) model | Account-based model |
| **Development & Upgrades** | Slow, focused on security and decentralization | Frequent upgrades (e.g., ETH 2.0, PoS transition) |
| **Privacy** | Pseudonymous but traceable with tools | Less privacy due to transparent account history |
| **Supply** | Capped at 21 million | No hard cap; issuance rate reduced with upgrades |
